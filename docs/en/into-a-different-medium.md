## A few steps to the next table

“I’ll show you when it’s ready.”

The message is still there. A few lines up, somebody has named the game. Somebody else asks if you can make a step that falls out from under the player. A few lines down, everybody’s deciding what to have for dinner. There’s no link in between.

Thirty seconds at the keyboard might have told you whether the falling step was any fun. Those thirty seconds never happened.

One of our favorite things at a hackathon is hearing someone say, “It’s running!” People look up from their own piles of cables and come over. But a room full of people can still spend a day working at separate tables. If everyone’s racing to finish a demo, you might not talk to the person next to you until they’re already packing up.

**We want to leave time for those few steps to the next table.** Take something that isn’t finished. Let someone else try it before you tell them how it works. Bring it back while you still have time to make a change.

## Could it pause for half a beat?

Say you’re tuning a robot to turn faster. Someone at the next table has done theater. They look over and ask, “Could it pause for half a beat before it turns?”

It isn’t a judging requirement. It might not even be a good suggestion. The nice part is that you can try it right there. Maybe it looks better with the pause. Maybe you go back to what you had. Either way, you wouldn’t have thought to try it on your own.

Light can change direction when it passes from one medium into another. We like the word *can*. Meeting someone you wouldn’t normally meet gives you another way to try something. What you do with it is up to you.

**We don’t want to tell light where to go. We want to create that different medium.**

## What this means for the schedule

We’re protecting a stretch of time to build, with room to try each other’s prototypes before the final demos. It doesn’t have to be a presentation. Turn your laptop around. Borrow a controller and play one round. Find one place somebody gets stuck, then go back to your table. If trying it makes you more confident in the original, that counts too.
