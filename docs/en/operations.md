# REFR/ACT operations playbook

[Playbook home](../../README.md) / [中文](../zh/operations.md)

This is the working plan, not a report on a past event. The [event schedule](schedule.md) is the public source of truth. This document explains who is responsible, how they check their work, and what to do when something fails. Do not announce an unconfirmed venue, capacity, registration rule or time.

## 1. Event boundaries

Day 1 is setup. Days 2–5 are in person. Participation is open regardless of school affiliation, student status or discipline. Teams choose their own problems and submit a prototype someone can try, with a short account of their decisions. Partner challenges, product trials and interviews are optional.

Before opening registration, confirm dates, rooms and capacity, team rules, access and safety requirements, registration and cancellation steps, and how judging and awards work.

## 2. Who is responsible

| Role | Deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Coordination | Master schedule, decisions, risk list and public updates. |
| Venue and safety | Floor plan, access, capacity, emergency contacts and room checks. |
| Participants and mentors | Arrival guide, team formation, mentor rota and a clear way to ask for help. |
| Judging and expo | Submission checklist, judge brief, conflicts policy, table numbers and finalist list. |
| Technical and supplies | Network and submission checks on an ordinary participant device, backup intake and equipment log. |
| Partnerships and finance | Written confirmations, on-site contact, resource log, separate accounting for challenge prizes and post-event reports. |

Give each deliverable one owner and one backup. A decision is complete when someone knows what to do and when to check that it works.

## 3. Work backward

| Checkpoint | What must be ready |
| --- | --- |
| Before opening registration | Dates, space, capacity, safety and finance confirmed; published eligibility, team rules and schedule. |
| Before confirming a partner | Resource amounts, restrictions and reporting terms are in writing; any company challenge has a one-page brief. |
| Final week | Test the network, power, check-in, submission and judging; send the arrival guide and confirm contacts, room assignments and backups. |
| After the event | Deliver the agreed report, return equipment, reconcile expenses and permissions, then review what to change next time. |

## 4. A small rehearsal

Have someone who did not write the instructions use an ordinary participant device to arrive, join the network, open the sample, submit and demo. Record what stumped them and who will fix it. A rehearsal is not the main event and does not award finalist slots.

## 5. Space, access and safety

- Confirm the work area, technical desks, expo tables, stage, interview area, rest space and clear exits. Keep interviews outside the judging route.
- Check capacity, power, accessible routes, dietary needs, equipment safety and on-site emergency contacts. Follow the published consent and supervision rules for minors.
- Visiting mentors and partner staff do not replace the people responsible for on-site safety.

## 6. Budget and partners

Track build supplies, venue, meals, equipment, publicity and awards separately. Record quantities, use, payment method and owner. Do not count an unsigned partnership as money available to spend.

Write down placements, resources, technical support, product trials and workshops. A company may bring an independent challenge with an agreed scope and deliverables. If it funds a challenge prize, keep that funding separate from the main awards; it does not affect main competition scores. See [work with REFR/ACT](partnerships.md).

## 7. Registration and participant communications

The registration page must say who can apply, what to submit, how teams form, when an application is confirmed, and where to ask about access, supervision or dietary needs. Collect only what is needed to run the event. Do not give partners the registration list.

Point confirmation messages, the arrival guide and on-site reminders to the same rules and schedule. No participant is required to take a company challenge or use a sponsor's tool.

## 8. Equipment and technical checks

- On an ordinary participant device, test networking, sign-in, tool permissions and credit use, and submission confirmation. Keep a fallback that works offline or through an alternate intake channel.
- Test projection, audio, timing and adapters. Check electrical routing, workbench safety and equipment checkout.
- For partner tools, offer a runnable example and check eligibility, regions, expiry, usage limits and data requirements.
- Record the device, steps, result, failure and person responsible for fixing it.

Run opt-in lightning interviews in a separate area at the announced appointment times, apart from expo and judging activities.

## 9. Submission and judging

Teams may build software, hardware, games or interactive prototypes. Ask for a brief project description, a core flow to demo, the major choices the team made, and test notes that include what failed. A working demo is not a production release.

**Day 4 is for submissions and the first round. Day 5 starts with the Open Expo, followed by finals.** Give every team an equal chance to demo during the first round. The expo is separate from judging, and every team that submits a project gets a booth. Publish submission rules, judging criteria, conflict handling and tie and late-submission procedures ahead of time. Use the same rubric across judges. Finalists demo and answer questions separately. Sponsor involvement and interviews do not affect scores.

## 10. On-site run of show

| Day | Organizer checks |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 · Setup | Test equipment, network, primary and backup submission; verify technical desks and demo tables. |
| Day 2 · Opening | Check in, point to the rules and help desk, then help creators find teammates. |
| Day 3 · Build | Protect the main build block. Plan informal prototype swaps with the Experience & Operations team and leave time afterward to revise before submission. Keep workshops and technical desks optional. |
| Day 4 · Submissions and first round | Remind teams of the cutoff, run the first round under the published rules and prepare the finalist list. |
| Day 5 · Open Expo and finals | For the morning Open Expo, set up a booth for every team that submits a project. Run finals and Q&A in the published order, then handle awards, equipment returns and closing. |

The live run sheet names a time, action, owner, backup and status. Any change that affects teams goes through a single announcement.

## 11. When something fails

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Network or tool outage | Switch to the agreed fallback and note affected teams. Apply any approved extension uniformly. |
| Submission outage | Use the backup intake, keep timestamps, and apply the same policy to every team. |
| Judge or mentor unavailable | Use the backup roster, then check conflicts and speaking time. |
| Health or safety issue | Contact the named on-site safety lead. People come before the timetable. |
| Resource or funding change | Stop unapproved spending or commitments, contact the partner and use checked substitutes. |

## 12. Closeout and retrospective

Check equipment returns, awards and partnership delivery. A useful report contains links, screenshots, verifiable data, resource use and anonymized feedback where agreed. Deliver challenge instructions and test notes according to the agreement. Do not publish private information or unauthorized work.

For each issue in the retrospective, record one change someone can make, who owns it and when it is due.
