# REFR/ACT operations playbook

[Playbook home](../../README.md) / [中文](../zh/operations.md)

This is the working plan, not a report on a past event. The [event schedule](schedule.md) is the public source of truth. This document explains who is responsible, how they check their work, and what to do when something fails. Mark any unconfirmed dates, venues, and registration details as pending in public materials.

## 1. Event boundaries

Day 0 is for setup and technical checks. Days 1–5 are in person. Applications are open regardless of school affiliation, student status, or discipline. Teams choose what to build and submit a prototype they can demo, with a short account of their decisions. Company tracks, product trials, and interviews are optional.

Before opening registration, confirm dates, rooms and capacity, team rules, access and safety requirements, registration and cancellation steps, and how judging and awards work.

## 2. Who is responsible

| Role | Deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Coordination | Master schedule, decisions, risk list and public updates. |
| Venue and safety | Floor plan, access, capacity, emergency contacts and room checks. |
| Participants and mentors | Arrival guide, team formation, mentor rota and a clear way to ask for help. |
| Judging and expo | Submission checklist, judge brief, conflicts policy, and expo table assignments. |
| Technical and supplies | Network and submission checks on an ordinary participant device, backup intake and equipment log. |
| Partnerships and finance | Written confirmations, on-site contact, resource log, separate accounting for challenge prizes and post-event reports. |

Give each deliverable one owner and one backup. A decision is complete when someone knows what to do and when to check that it works.

## 3. Work backward

| Checkpoint | What must be ready |
| --- | --- |
| Before opening registration | Dates, space, capacity, safety and finance confirmed; published eligibility, team rules and schedule. |
| Before confirming a partner | Sign the agreement covering scope, delivery criteria, resources, usage terms, and reporting. Prepare a separate public brief for each company track with its direction, entry requirements, judging rules, and award. |
| Final week | Test the network, power, check-in, submission and judging; send the arrival guide and confirm contacts, room assignments and backups. |
| After the event | Deliver agreed partner reports within 10 business days of closing; return equipment, reconcile expenses, verify permissions, and review what to change next time. |

## 4. A small rehearsal

Have someone who did not write the instructions use an ordinary participant device to arrive, join the network, open the sample, submit and demo. Record what stumped them and who will fix it. Rehearsal does not replace formal submission or judging.

## 5. Space, access and safety

- Confirm the work area, technical desks, expo tables, stage, interview area, rest space and clear exits. Keep interviews outside the judging route.
- Check capacity, power, accessible routes, dietary needs, equipment safety and on-site emergency contacts. Follow the published consent and supervision rules for minors.
- Visiting mentors and partner staff do not replace the people responsible for on-site safety.

## 6. Budget and partners

Track build supplies, venue, meals, equipment, publicity and awards separately. Record quantities, use, payment method and owner. Do not count an unsigned partnership as money available to spend.

Record placements, resources, technical support, product trials, and workshops in the agreement. A company can run a standalone [public track](tracks.md) with one open direction and its own published rules. It funds its award separately with cash or equivalent products and appoints its own judges. Track prizes and judging are separate from the main competition. See [work with REFR/ACT](partnerships.md).

## 7. Registration and participant communications

The application window runs from opening to the deadline, before the event. Publish what to submit, how applications are reviewed, when notices go out, how teams form, and whom to ask about accessibility, minors’ participation, or dietary needs. Review why people want to join and what they want to explore. Past work is optional; school affiliation is not a criterion. The organizers confirm places after review. Collect only what the event needs and keep the registration list private.

Confirmation messages and the arrival guide cover check-in time and entrance, what to bring, venue hours, meals, accessibility, and how to get help on site. Link the guide and on-site reminders to the same rules and schedule. People may apply without teammates and form a team on Day 1. Teams may enter multiple company tracks; no one is required to use a partner tool.

## 8. Equipment and technical checks

- On an ordinary participant device, test networking, sign-in, tool permissions and credit use, and submission confirmation. Keep a fallback that works offline or through an alternate intake channel.
- Test projection, audio, timing and adapters. Check electrical routing and safe equipment use; prepare the checkout log.
- For partner tools, offer a runnable example and check eligibility, regions, expiry, usage limits and data requirements.
- Record the device, steps, result, failure and person responsible for fixing it.

## 9. Submission and judging

Teams may build software, hardware, games or interactive prototypes. Ask for a brief project description, a core flow to demo, the major choices the team made, and test notes that include what failed. A working demo is not a production release.

**The all-team Expo and main judging take place on Day 5 in the morning.** Give each team that submits a project a table and the same opportunity to demo. Publish submission rules, judging criteria, conflict handling, and tie and late-submission procedures in advance. Main competition judges use the same rubric; company tracks are evaluated separately. Partner tool use, track entry, and recruiting conversations do not affect main competition scores.

## 10. On-site run of show

| Day | Organizer checks |
| --- | --- |
| Day 0 · Setup | Check the venue, equipment, network, primary and backup submission paths, and the floor plan. |
| Day 1 · Arrival and opening | Check in participants and run the Partner Showcase before the opening ceremony; publish the arrival guide and open team roster submissions and building. |
| Day 2 · Build and workshops | Run optional workshops and technical desks. Protect a continuous block of build time. |
| Day 3 · Build and exchange | Run workshops and cross-team prototype testing, with time to revise afterward. Open opt-in lightning interview bookings. |
| Day 4 · Team lock | Lock rosters, remind teams of the project deadline, close interview booking, and send meeting slots. |
| Day 5 · Expo and closing | Close submissions and verify tables. Run the all-team Expo and judging in the morning. Set up interviews in a separate area alongside afternoon workshops, then record awards at the evening closing. |

The live run sheet names a time, action, owner, backup and status. Any change that affects teams goes through a single announcement.

## 11. When something fails

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Network or tool outage | Switch to the agreed fallback and note affected teams. Apply any approved extension uniformly. |
| Submission outage | Use the backup intake, keep timestamps, and apply the same policy to every team. |
| Judge or mentor unavailable | Use the backup roster, then check conflicts and speaking time. |
| Health or safety issue | Contact the named on-site safety lead. People come before the timetable. |
| Resource or funding change | Stop unapproved spending or commitments, contact the partner and use checked substitutes. |

## 12. Closeout and retrospective

Check equipment returns, awards and partnership delivery. Include placement links, screenshots, and verifiable data. Report platform metrics and on-site check-ins separately. Summarize resource use, anonymized feedback, track entries, and talent introductions as agreed. Share work and personal information only within the scope of permission given.

For each issue in the retrospective, record one change someone can make, who owns it and when it is due.
